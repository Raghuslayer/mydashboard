## 🚀 Getting Started

### Step 1: Add Your First Achievement
1. Click **"Inspiration → Achievement Jar"** in sidebar
2. Click **"Add Achievement"** button
3. Enter title: *"Started my Achievement Jar"*
4. Add description: *"Taking control of my mindset and documenting my journey"*
5. Choose 🚀 Rocket icon
6. Choose Orange-Red gradient
7. Click **"Add Achievement"**

### Step 2: Add Past Victories
Think back over your life. What are you proud of?
- Last week
- Last month
- Last year
- Last 5 years
- Childhood
- School
- Career
- Personal life

**Add at least 10 achievements to start.** The more you add, the more powerful your jar becomes.

### Step 3: Make It a Habit
- **Daily**: Add today's wins (even small ones)
- **Weekly**: Review and add week's achievements
- **Monthly**: Reflect on month's progress
- **Yearly**: Celebrate annual growth

### Step 4: Use It When You Need It
- Before challenges
- During tough times
- When feeling low
- When doubting yourself
- When needing energy
- When wanting inspiration

## 💡 Pro Tips

### 1. Be Specific
❌ "Did well at work"
✅ "Closed $50K deal after 3 months of negotiations, proving I can handle enterprise sales"

### 2. Include Emotions
❌ "Ran 5K"
✅ "Ran my first 5K! Felt like I could fly. Proved to myself I'm not 'not a runner' anymore."

### 3. Add Context
❌ "Finished project"
✅ "Finished project despite 3 major setbacks, team member leaving, and tight deadline. Learned I thrive under pressure."

### 4. Mix Sizes
Don't just add huge wins. Small daily victories matter too:
- Made bed every day this week
- Drank 8 glasses of water
- Went to bed on time
- Kept promise to myself

### 5. Add Immediately
Don't wait! Add achievements right after they happen while the emotion is fresh.

### 6. Review Regularly
Your jar is only powerful if you use it. Make it part of your routine.

### 7. Share Selectively
Some achievements are just for you. Others you might want to share with accountability partners or mentors.

## 🎯 The Cookie Jar Philosophy

### David Goggins' Original Concept
When Goggins faces a challenge, he mentally reaches into his "Cookie Jar" - a collection of past victories. Each "cookie" is a reminder: "I've done hard things before. I can do this."
